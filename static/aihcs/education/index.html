Education Program

Undergraduate and graduate pathways into AI for healthcare computing and systems.

Healthcare-specific AI systems need engineers who are fluent on both sides of the clinic door — comfortable with silicon, signal processing and systems software, and equally comfortable with the constraints a hospital, a device on a patient's body, or a clinician's workflow actually impose. The Center's education program is built to produce that engineer, at both the undergraduate and graduate level, through coursework, hands-on projects, and direct participation in the Center's own research.

Undergraduate

Undergraduates working with the Center pursue a concentration or certificate that layers AI-for-healthcare content on top of a standard engineering or computer science degree, rather than replacing it. The emphasis throughout is project-based: students take a clinical problem — a wearable sensor, an assistive device, a diagnostic model — from a rough specification to working hardware or software they can demonstrate, so that by graduation they have built something, not just studied how it is built.

Graduate

At the master's and doctoral level, students can pursue a concentration that draws on the Center's full research stack — hardware design for medical AI applications, medical robotics, signal processing and control for physiological data, and the computing frameworks that hold multi-institution clinical AI systems together. Graduate students are typically embedded in an active Center research project from early in their program, working alongside faculty and, where the project calls for it, clinical collaborators at UPMC.

Courses

Core and related coursework, organised by the part of the stack it addresses. The listing is being finalised: the topics below are what each group will cover, and course numbers and titles will follow once the participating departments have confirmed them.

Devices, Chips & Architecture

  • Biosensor and low-power circuit design
  • Computer architecture for embedded and edge AI

Signal Processing, Control & Robotics

  • Physiological signal processing
  • Medical robotics
  • Closed-loop and assistive control

AI, Software & Security

  • Machine learning and foundation models
  • Federated and privacy-preserving systems

Clinical & Domain Context

  • Clinical and biomedical context, cross-listed with the School of Medicine or the School of Health and Rehabilitation Sciences

Student Research Opportunities

Coursework is only one half of the program. Students who want to go further can work directly on Center research.

Undergraduate Research

Undergraduates can join Center research through independent study, through senior design and capstone projects sponsored by Center faculty, and through paid summer research positions as they are offered. To ask about any of these, write to the Center.
